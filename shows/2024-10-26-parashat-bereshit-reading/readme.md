## Chapters / Description

This last Shabbat (October 26, 2024) was the first reading of the Torah cycle which just began (following as it does the end of Tabernacles). This portion contains Genesis 1 - 6:8. Join me as I read the entire parashah in one go, straight through. If I were braver I would have read from the unvocalized text, which actually would not be too difficult given I am quite familiar with these passages, but given I am starting some things out with such readings, I went the easier route and am reading from the vocalized.

At the same time I have gone to great lengths to capture a beautiful physical text from which I am reading. At the same time I also put the Hebrew and English translation so you can follow along. (It's been a long journey learning how to do all of this in Premier Pro, but even bigger is I've had a ton to learn to do proper audio processing in Audition)

May the LORD bless you and I as we meditate on His Word. I'm reminded of the beautiful saying from Pirkei Avot (Sayings of the Fathers):

הֲפֹךְ בָּהּ וַהֲפֹךְ בָּהּ, דְּכֹלָּא בָהּ. וּבָהּ תֶּחֱזֵי

Ben Bag Bag said: Turn it over, and again turn it over, for all is therein. And look into it; And become gray and old therein; And do not move away from it, for you have no better portion than it.

https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.5.22

While I do not comment on things here, one thing I'll highlight that captured me as I read this passage a few times through (the recorded reading here was cold-turkey and the first read through, so pardon a few stumbling, but since then I've read it more in preparing this). Which is the heart of God, of YHWH Elohim, who was so gracious and yet broken in his speech to Cain (in my view, this is one of the most gracious places in Scripture, where we see the compassionate and understanding heart of God, who is for us and routing for us, but WE indeed have our own part to play, which is what God told Cain, but YOU must master that sin crouching at your door). How He tried to warn Adam and Eve before their fall, and how he was searching for them afterwards (aiyecha?! WHERE ARE YOU?!). After which he hand prepared tunics for them, with the FIRST shed blood in this world, of an animal, the first sacrifice. And finally, at the start of the flood, as it says essentially He was cut to the heart because every thought we were thinking down here was 'raq ra'h' / ONLY EVIL all the day.

What also stood out to me was in creation week, and at the end of the fall in ch. 3, the plurality of God in speaking to Himself. I know very well that both sides, traditional Judaism and Christianity, can give reasonable arguments for their side on such things, I know, I know. But consider this, my friends: does even traditional Judaism totally forbid some form of complexity in the being and nature of God, so long as let's say that remains transcendent? (Of course that's the catch, isn't it: Jesus didn't remain august and transcendant, he came down into our lowliness and into the filth of this world, and worse, became a little one of us in body... but maybe that is the "Word", the Memra, the immenation of God that always has gone forth from His transcendant beyondedness, eh?! What if God has always wanted a way and means to "come down" to our level? The Word that comes out of His august mouth, what a sweet breath and vocalic wonder, always had to leave the perfect realm of His Mind and Utterly Beyond Dimension, into a created realm, always a descent)

It was not just a "wind" blowing on the face of creation at the world's start, but a "Holy" wind or spirit. Maybe one can say this was just a "divine wind," maybe. But perhaps we could also say things have already gotten more complex. One doesn't have to count up to 10 sephirot to accept some form of complexity in Elohim. 

Without Yeshua' / Jesus, I would know nothing and be nothing, so I accept reality according to that great Rabbon's teaching, and I understand that's not where all of you come from. I am biased. That said:

וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים הֵן הָאָדָם הָיָה כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ

 (https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.3.22)

And YHWH Elohim said: Look, the Man has become like one of us...

כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ - Like one of Us / Like one from amongst Us ...

... without any religious or heady scholastic filter to undo these plain words, it sounds like there are more than one of them. Yet the person speaking here is "YHWH Elohim"...

Much to ponder! May God's Holy Spirit help us, even as he brooded originally on those waters. Shalom, Nicholas
